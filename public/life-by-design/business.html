Where the starting numbers come from

This planner adapts The Millionaire Real Estate Agent (Keller, Jenks & Papasan, 2004), figures 18-19 on printed page 157. The $180,000 GCI example has $21,000 cost of sales and $59,300 operating expenses, including $20,000 salaries. These are historical illustrations, not current industry averages or guaranteed results.

The solo preset removes the salary allowance. Initial non-salary operating budgets are scaled from that column's proportions to your initial income goal; the monthly dollar budgets then stay fixed until you change them. Lead generation starts at 10% of GCI. The combined base cost-of-sales allowance starts at $21,000 / $180,000 = 11.6667%. It is not a recommended brokerage split. Entering your actual brokerage costs replaces that allowance, rather than adding to it.

Staffing levels are separate from the chart's production columns. Admin pay starts at an illustrative $4,000/month per person, including all employer costs. This is an editable app assumption, not a book benchmark or a wage quote. Level 4 retains two admins and adds buyer support. A showing specialist is an alternative staffing scenario. Buyer-agent pay starts at the book's illustrative 50% of assigned buyer GCI; showing support starts at an app assumption of $500 per assisted closed buyer side.

Figure 34 on printed page 183 supplies the starting conversions: sellers 80% appointment-to-agreement and 65% agreement-to-closing; buyers 65% and 80%. These are planning benchmarks, not promises. An appointment is a buyer/seller consultation, not a showing. Commission dollars start at an illustrative $10,000 per represented side. Use your own negotiated compensation.

The buyer/seller mix is a share of GCI, not a share of transactions. Percentage fees use gross commission, not the amount left after another fee. Caps are one business-wide annual amount per fee, not separate caps for every team member. Personal taxes are already in your life target and are not added again here.

The financial model retains fractional expected annual sales. Sales commitments and monthly appointments round up separately for each side. Weekly commitments annualize those rounded monthly goals over your chosen working weeks and round each side up again. Rounding creates a cushion; it does not change the underlying GCI calculation. Staffing does not automatically improve conversion rates or guarantee capacity.
